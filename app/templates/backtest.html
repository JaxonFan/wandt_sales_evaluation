Book profit = the accounts the rep works this cycle, at their work-share — and the last-year column is those SAME accounts (even if someone else sold them then), so every row subtracts cleanly: net gap = book this yr − same accounts last yr. Growth pay = {{ '%.0f'|format(rate*100) }}% × new ground above the book's previous cumulative peak; a red (negative) month pays $0 and is never clawed back.

Pay on collection — bonus is only payable on money that actually came in

The cycle's earned bonus (all three pieces) is a target; what's payable now scales by the share of the rep's cycle billing that has actually been collected (per the latest receivables upload). As invoices pay, the % rises and the remainder releases — a true-up that never overpays. Voided or written-off invoices never count.

How to read this — accounts, not salespeople

We compare the accounts a rep works today against what those same accounts did a year ago — no matter who worked them then. We deliberately do not compare a rep's own sales this year to their own sales last year, because accounts get reassigned. A real example:

Jan 2025An Cao sold NEW BEN CITY 檳城 — $4,119 profit that month.
2026The account was reassigned to Vanessa.
Wrong wayCompare An Cao 2026 vs his own 2025 sales → the $4,119 he no longer works counts against him, and Vanessa gets it as free "growth" with no baseline. Reassignment, not performance.
This modelNEW BEN CITY moves with its history: it leaves An Cao's book including its $4,119 baseline, and Vanessa inherits the account with that baseline — she only earns if it does better than $4,119. Nobody gains or loses from a handoff.

Same principle protects against churn of people: accounts once served by staff who left (e.g. Maple Tran) keep their full history, so whoever inherits them isn't paid for "growth" that is really just a handover.
